Can AI safely prepare a startup launch brief?

I gave three models the same six fictional company documents. The task was not simply to find contradictions. It was to keep disputed claims out of anything a writer might publish as fact.

The question

Could a writer trust the “confirmed” section?

Founders and content teams increasingly use AI to turn internal information into launch copy. Those internal documents do not always agree. A pricing sheet can conflict with a founder’s notes. Security documentation can contradict a customer-facing claim.

I wanted to know whether each model could identify those conflicts and produce a brief a writer could safely use without repeating disputed information.

Method

Same evidence. Same prompt. One rubric.

I used a fictional startup to reduce the chance that a model already knew the company. Gemini created six source documents and a private answer key with ten planted issues, then took no part in the evaluation.

GPT 5.6 Sol Max, Fable 5 Max and Grok 4.5 Speed received the same documents and prompt. Their answers were anonymised before grading. AI graders assisted, but I checked every conclusion against the original documents and made the final assessment.

Why I restarted the first attemptI noticed that the model used to create the original materials later produced the strongest answer. I could not prove bias, but the setup raised a fairness question, so I treated it as a pilot and began again.

Findings

Finding a contradiction is not enough.

Fable and Grok identified major conflicts, then still placed some of the disputed information under confirmed facts. A writer scanning only that section could publish a claim the same response later admitted was uncertain.

Enterprise usage

The pricing document capped Enterprise generation at 250,000 words per month. The founder’s notes called it unlimited. All three models noticed. Fable still put the capped figure under confirmed pricing.

Security and data processing

The founder claimed customer data never reached external AI providers. The security document said some plans routed prompt payloads through OpenAI and Anthropic. None of that could safely be resolved without clarification.

A problem the answer key missed

The security document referred to a “Standard” plan that did not exist in pricing. GPT was the only model to ask whether it meant “Starter.” My review found that the answer key had silently assumed the two were the same.

Result

GPT was the safest in this one scenario.

GPT 5.6 Sol Max97/100
Fable 5 Max92/100
Grok 4.5 Speed80/100
Practical takeawayWhen reviewing an AI-generated brief, check what it labels as safe—not only whether it mentioned the problem somewhere.

Limits

What the scores do not mean.

This was one fictional company, one launch scenario and one attempt per model. The scores describe performance in this test; they are not a universal model ranking.

The more important result is the review method: define what “safe” means before testing, use the same evidence for every model, and return to the original sources before accepting any grader’s conclusion.
